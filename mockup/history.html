The history of ovarian cancer research.

Major clinical and scientific milestones, alongside the moments from my own research where something clicked. The line at the bottom is mine — it grows as I go.

Clinical & scientific milestones
1970 — present
1971
Platinum compounds enter cancer treatment
Cisplatin's anti-tumor activity is confirmed, laying the groundwork for platinum-based chemotherapy that remains first-line treatment today.
1981
CA-125 is identified
The CA-125 antigen is discovered and soon becomes the standard blood biomarker for monitoring ovarian cancer, despite its known limitations.
1994
BRCA1 is cloned
The BRCA1 gene is identified and linked to hereditary breast and ovarian cancer risk, followed by BRCA2 in 1995 — reshaping genetic counseling and screening.
2011
TCGA publishes the ovarian cancer atlas
The Cancer Genome Atlas releases a comprehensive genomic analysis of high-grade serous ovarian carcinoma, becoming the foundational public dataset for ML research in the field.
2014
PARP inhibitors are approved
Olaparib is approved for BRCA-mutated ovarian cancer, the first therapy to directly exploit a genetic vulnerability identified through molecular research.
2017
The fallopian tube origin theory gains ground
Growing evidence suggests many high-grade serous cases originate in the fallopian tube rather than the ovary, reshaping prevention and early-detection research.
